Follow my lead. I’ll show ya who’s the ‘strongest’! Jungle Pocket · ジャングルポケット

Full of mischief and a mouth that likes to boast, Jungle Pocket, “Pokke” to anyone brave enough, has exactly one ambition: to be the strongest runner who ever lived. Not the prettiest. Not the most graceful. The strongest.

She didn’t come up through the academy system. She came up on the street, running freestyle races at the head of a pack of ruffians who’d follow her anywhere. Then she watched Fuji Kiseki run, one blaze of speed, and decided the official turf was where she belonged.

Her bros still turn up to cheer. And when she wins a G1, she doesn’t smile politely and wave. She roars.

Read the outfit. Yellow and white cropped jacket taking its colors straight from her ear cuff; light-green flashes on the sleeves; a black crop top with a yellow bowtie dead centre. Navy skirt, lime suspenders clipped to the belt, and a length of leopard-print fur hanging off the left hip. Then the tell: the right sock slouches, the left is pulled tight. Nothing about it is regulation, and the steel-toe boots with leopard heel panels are the point.

Beginning Of
A New Era

The franchise’s first theatrical film opened in Japan on 24 May 2024, and it handed the lead to Jungle Pocket.

It is, in the most literal sense, her story: the delinquent who watched Fuji Kiseki run and enrolled at Tracen; the Classic season shaped by Agnes Tachyon and Dantsu Flame; the roar. She carries it as co-protagonist alongside Narita Top Road: her roommate, and the lead of Road to the Top, which shares the same continuity.

She’s voiced by Yuri Fujimoto, and sings Saikyo ROARING (“strongest roaring”) as her solo.

The Real-Life
Legend That
Inspired Her

A bay colt with a narrow white blaze, sixteen hands high, bred at Northern Farm in Hokkaido and named, of all things, after an episode of the NHK children’s programme Okaasan to Issho.

He was by Tony Bin, the Irish-bred stayer who won the 1988 Prix de l’Arc de Triomphe before heading to Japan to become one of the most influential imported sires of his generation. His dam, Dance Charmer, never raced a day in her life.

He carried the colors of Yomoji Saito and was trained throughout by Sakae Watanabe, the same owner, trainer and jockey behind Fuji Kiseki, the brilliant colt whose Classic campaign injury cut short. Jungle Pocket would go on to win the Derby that Fuji Kiseki never got to contest.

Thirteen starts. Five wins. Two of them the biggest races in the country, seven months apart, at three years old.

The Derby

第68回 東京優駿

Agnes Tachyon, the colt who had beaten him twice, was gone, a career-ending injury ruling him out of the Classic he was favourite for. What remained was still a proper field: Dantsu Flame and the American-bred Kurofune.

Jungle Pocket drew the outermost gate of eighteen and went off the 2.3 favourite anyway. Koichi Tsunoda brought him through the soft ground to win by a length and a half. Then he did the thing that would define him: pulling up after the line, he threw his head and let out a neighing fit so loud and so sustained it became part of the record. The roar Cygames would later hand to his Umamusume counterpart, note for note.

The Japan Cup

第21回 ジャパンカップ

Fifteen runners, 115,196 people, and a genuinely international field: America, Britain, Germany and Hong Kong all represented, with Meisho Doto, Stay Gold and Narita Top Road heading the home defence.

At the top of the market stood T. M. Opera O: seven G1s already banked, six of them consecutive across an unbeaten 2000 season. Jungle Pocket, with Olivier Peslier up for the first time, was second favourite at 4.2. Peslier settled him near the back, swung wide in the straight, and ran the champion down in the final strides, by a neck. The Opera O dynasty ended right there.

“I’m on top of the world. Jungle Pocket is the Derby winner and I am really proud of the way he has run today in this field. I was bumped a little at the start, and he then raced quite a long way back, but he settled well and I got a good run through.” Olivier Peslier, after the race
